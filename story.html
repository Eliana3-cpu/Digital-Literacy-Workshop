Our Story

I grew up in Ethiopia with limited access to reliable electricity, internet, and digital devices. In fifth grade, I moved to a school that had computers and a stable internet connection — and that access changed what I could actually do with things I'd previously only read about in books. One of the first things I searched for was Stanford University, something I'd only encountered through books and imagination up to that point.

Around seventh grade, I worked on a group project building an HTML website about nonprofit organizations in Addis Ababa and how they used technology to organize their work. Not long after, I completed Yeneta's introductory coding and digital-literacy program, where I learned the basics of coding and Scratch, and started making my own small games and animations.

In summer 2025, I spent about two months working with Ethiopia Reads, teaching English, math, science, and basic digital literacy to students. That experience showed me just how wide the technology-access gap can be for students who have little or no access to a device outside of school or a community program.

That's what led to this initiative: running digital-literacy and introductory-coding workshops, in partnership with two nonprofit organizations including Ethiopia Reads, for students who are starting from the same place I once did.
